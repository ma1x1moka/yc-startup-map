description: A shifting label rather than a fixed technology. It points at whatever machines have most recently learned to do impressively — today, large language models.
---

The word has moved targets for seventy years. Optical character recognition, chess engines, spam filters and route planners were each called AI while they were hard, and each quietly stopped being called AI once they worked reliably. What survives the rebranding gets a plain engineering name; what is still surprising keeps the label.

For everyday work the term is close to useless as a technical description, because it says nothing about what is actually running. When someone says an AI wrote the code, the load-bearing facts are which [model](./Model.md), inside which [harness](./Harness.md), with what in its [context](./Context.md). Reach for those words instead.

_Avoid:_ Treating "AI" as a capability claim. It describes a category's reputation, not what a specific system can do.

_Usage:_

"Can the AI handle this refactor?"

"Depends entirely on the model and what's in its context — 'AI' isn't a spec."
