Atomic Alchemy is constructing a bank of small modular reactors that will serve as a dedicated, scalable facility for producing radioisotopes used in nuclear medicine, such as the Molybdenum‑99 needed for diagnostics and cancer treatments. The current global supply of Molybdenum‑99 relies on six aging, government‑run research reactors abroad, five of which are 45‑65 years old, creating a fragile and lengthy supply chain.

By creating the world’s first privately‑owned reactors focused on isotope production, Atomic Alchemy aims to shorten lead times and enable more reliable access to materials for clinical trials and treatments. The company, based in Idaho Falls, ID with a team of eight, was acquired, reflecting the strategic value of its hard‑tech approach to medical device supply.

_Usage:_

"How does Atomic Alchemy’s private reactor model solve the supply‑chain problems of Molybdenum‑99 compared to the existing government‑run reactors?"

"Atomic Alchemy plans to operate its own small modular reactors solely for radioisotope production, eliminating dependence on the six foreign, aging research reactors that currently supply 80% of Molybdenum‑99. By owning the production facility, it can scale output, reduce transport delays, and provide a more stable source for diagnostics and cancer therapies."
