**Website:** http://edlyft.com | **YC:** https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/edlyft

Edlyft built software that allowed blue-chip enterprises to train college computer science students on essential workforce skills, aiming to make new graduates productive from day one. The company addressed the common issue where new grad engineers take six to twelve months to ramp up, offering an alternative to relying solely on experienced engineers for onboarding. Notable users included Google, Dropbox, Tinder, and LinkedIn, who utilized the platform to prepare students for their specific roles prior to hiring.

Backed by Kleiner Perkins, Kapor Capital, and Y Combinator, Edlyft operated with a small team of five based in San Francisco and remote locations. The company was founded by CS graduates from Yale and Stanford who had previously worked at Facebook, Google, LinkedIn, and Bain. Edlyft has since become inactive.

_Usage:_

"How does Edlyft reduce the time it takes for new grad engineers to become productive?"

"Instead of waiting for new hires to ramp up on the job over six to twelve months, Edlyft trains college CS students on essential skills before they are hired. This allows enterprises to onboard candidates who are already prepared for the specific requirements of the role, reducing the initial salary cost associated with training inexperienced engineers."
