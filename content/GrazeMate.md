Builds autonomous drone systems that monitor cattle herds across large grazing areas — tracking individual animals, detecting health issues, managing grazing rotation, and providing the livestock data that ranchers currently collect on horseback or ATVs.

Cattle ranching across the US involves monitoring animals spread across tens of thousands of acres. A rancher with 500 head of cattle can spend hours daily on patrol that produces limited data. GrazeMate replaces that patrol with continuous autonomous coverage and computer vision identification of each animal.

The practical problems being solved: early disease detection (an animal behaving differently before showing physical symptoms), grazing rotation optimization (moving herds before pasture is overgrazed), and accurate headcount (theft and stray detection).

The market is large and ignored: US cattle industry is $100B+, technology adoption is low, and precision agriculture software has focused on crops, not livestock.

1,210 Twitter likes at launch — high engagement for an agricultural tech product, suggesting significant rancher interest in the category.

GrazeMate sits in the same W26 hardware cluster as [Pocket](./Pocket.md) and [Fort](./Fort.md) — all three bet that purpose-built physical devices extract value that software-only solutions can't reach.

_Usage:_

"How does this differ from existing precision ag software?"

"Precision agriculture software has historically focused on crops rather than livestock. Instead of a software-only layer, GrazeMate deploys autonomous drones with computer vision to handle the physical challenge of tracking cattle across tens of thousands of acres. It replaces manual horseback or ATV patrols with continuous aerial coverage, enabling individual animal identification, automated headcount for theft detection, optimized grazing rotations, and early disease detection based on subtle behavioral changes."
