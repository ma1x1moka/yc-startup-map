Volumetric is a biotech company based in Houston, Texas, that develops living multicellular tissues using breakthrough 3D bioprinting technologies. The company, which was acquired after its founding in 2018, focuses on replicating discrete functions of the human body to improve basic mechanistic biology, laboratory testing, and drug development. Its technology, featured on the cover of Science, enables the creation of human tissues with complex blood vessel networks.

In the short term, Volumetric leverages this bioprinting capability to build patient-specific tissues for drug safety and efficacy testing. The process involves taking patient cells from a biopsy, integrating them into proprietary 3D printed tissues, and injecting drug compounds through the blood vessel network to mimic actual human drug delivery. This approach aims to provide rigorous, reproducible, and predictive outcomes for diseases such as cancer.

The long-term vision for the company is to produce functional tissue and organ replacements for vital organs, including the lung, liver, pancreas, kidney, and heart, using the patient's own cells. As a small team of five, the company operates at the intersection of healthcare and industrial bio, seeking to drastically improve patient outcomes through advanced tissue engineering.

_Usage:_

"How does Volumetric's bioprinting approach differ from standard 2D cell culture in drug testing?"

"Standard 2D cell cultures lack the structural complexity of real human tissue, often leading to poor predictions of drug safety and efficacy. Volumetric's method uses 3D bioprinting to create tissues with actual blood vessel networks, allowing drug compounds to be injected and delivered in a way that mimics the human body. This results in more reproducible and predictive outcomes compared to traditional laboratory testing methods."
